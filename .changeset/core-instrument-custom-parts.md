Draw custom parts in the Instrument level: the `instrument` material's scene takes `mesh` profiles (inline triangles, or a reference to a glB/glTF by URL or bytes, with `scale`, `offset` and a declared `bounds`) and the `custom` part kind, with every property of the parametric parts (`material`, `at`, `pose`, `caster`, `emitter`, `state`). A mesh is validated with the path and the reason of the first problem (non-finite values, a degenerate triangle, indices out of range, missing normals unless `"compute"`, more than 16,384 vertices or 32,768 triangles, a declared box that does not hold it) and every triangle is wound against its normals, so no top is lit as if it faced inward. Containment (the box rule over the whole travel) runs on the mesh's own vertices: a part that cannot stay in its box steps its object down to Relief with the `containment` diagnostic naming it, never clipped. A reference is loaded by core, outside any frame, into a per-engine cache under a 32 MiB budget with a small glB reader (positions, normals, `TEXCOORD_0` and indices; no Draco, meshopt, quantization, sparse accessors, external buffers or skins): while it loads, and if it fails, the part is not drawn and every other part of the layer is, no object steps down and no scene is rebuilt; the triangles replace the kind's placeholder mesh in place when they arrive. `state().instrument.layers[].customParts` reports each part as `loading`, `ready` or `failed` with its reason, and `state().instrument.assets` the store. A mesh's face caster is the footprint of its posed convex hull, its height capped so that the shadow stays inside the object's box; custom triangles over 1,000,000 per layer step the last objects down (`budget`). New modules `instrument-mesh`, `instrument-gltf` and `instrument-assets`; `InstrumentMeshProfile` and `InstrumentMeshSource` are exported types. GPU stays on in every case.
